# Macedonian Competency Syllabus

## Purpose and authority

This syllabus is a flexible roadmap for choosing coherent next skills. It does not record current progress, assign fixed lesson numbers, or override tracker evidence.

- `macedonian_vocabulary.md` and `macedonian_grammar.md` are the sole source of the learner's demonstrated state and current priorities.
- `macedonian_lesson_structure.md` controls lesson delivery.
- Select the earliest useful competency whose prerequisites are sufficiently demonstrated, while continuing spaced retrieval from earlier units.
- It is acceptable to move forward with skills marked developing when the new material reinforces them, but do not stack several unstable dependencies in one lesson.
- Do not mark a unit complete in this file. Evidence belongs in the trackers.

## Progression principles

- Spiral rather than finish-and-forget: every lesson retrieves older material.
- Teach one meaningful grammatical demand per normal lesson.
- Introduce related contrasts together when separating them would cause confusion.
- Use practical vocabulary themes to support the grammar target.
- Progress from recognition to supported production, transformation, independent production, and conversation.
- Revisit a competency after a delay before treating repeated success as secure.
- Weekly cumulative reviews sample the entire demonstrated history, prioritising weak and stale items.

## Competency sequence

### 1. Foundations and personal information

**Abilities**

- Greet someone and exchange basic wellbeing.
- Say one's name, origin, residence, preferences, and immediate activities.
- Omit subject pronouns naturally in familiar first-person phrases.

**Core patterns**

- `сум`, `не сум`
- `јас сум од...`
- `живеам во...`
- `сакам + noun`
- `сакам да + present verb`

**Vocabulary themes**

Greetings, countries and cities, basic food and drink, common activities.

### 2. Present-tense people and actions

**Prerequisite**

Reliable first-person use of several familiar verbs.

**Abilities**

- Contrast first and second person.
- Use `тој` and `таа` together and understand that present-tense verbs do not change for masculine versus feminine subjects.
- Later add `ние`, `вие`, and `тие` once singular forms are reasonably stable.
- Ask and answer who performs a familiar action.

**Core patterns**

- `одам / одиш / оди`
- `пијам / пиеш / пие`
- `јадам / јадеш / јаде`
- `купувам / купуваш / купува`

**Vocabulary themes**

Daily routines, people, shopping actions, food categories.

### 3. Questions and short exchanges

**Prerequisite**

Basic statements with familiar present-tense verbs.

**Abilities**

- Ask and answer `што` and `каде` questions.
- Add `кој / која / кое` only after noun gender is introduced.
- Recognise neutral question order while accepting valid natural variation.
- Sustain a coherent 3–4-turn exchange.

**Core patterns**

- `Што пиеш?`
- `Каде одиш?`
- `Што јаде таа?`
- simple yes/no questions and short answers

**Vocabulary themes**

Meals, destinations, everyday choices.

### 4. Places, movement, and source

**Prerequisite**

Familiar movement and shopping verbs.

**Abilities**

- Contrast destination, location, and source.
- Use common idiomatic place phrases without overgeneralising a single preposition.
- Combine place phrases with time and frequency.

**Core patterns**

- `во + place`
- `на работа`, `на пазарот`
- `од + source`
- `дома` without `во`

**Vocabulary themes**

Home, work, shops, neighbourhood places, transport.

### 5. Nouns and definiteness

**Prerequisite**

A practical stock of familiar nouns.

**Abilities**

- Recognise basic masculine, feminine, and neuter noun patterns.
- Form and understand common suffixed definite articles.
- Avoid treating every ending as an infallible gender rule.

**Core patterns**

- masculine examples such as `пазар / пазарот`
- feminine examples such as `продавница / продавницата`
- neuter examples introduced through useful familiar nouns

**Vocabulary themes**

Household objects, foods, places, personal belongings.

### 6. Description and possession

**Prerequisite**

Basic noun gender and definiteness.

**Abilities**

- Describe people and objects with common adjectives.
- Begin adjective agreement in controlled examples.
- Express simple possession and family relationships.

**Core patterns**

- common adjective pairs and neuter forms
- `мој / моја / мое`
- possession with `има` and natural noun phrases

**Vocabulary themes**

Family, appearance, colours, sizes, common objects.

### 7. Time, frequency, and routines

**Prerequisite**

Stable present-tense sentences and basic question forms.

**Abilities**

- Talk about days, clock time, repeated routines, and sequence.
- Place time and frequency words naturally without treating one order as mandatory.
- Contrast what different people usually or sometimes do.

**Core patterns**

- `денес`, `утре`
- `секогаш`, `понекогаш`
- days of the week and basic clock-time expressions
- simple sequence words

**Vocabulary themes**

Weekly routines, appointments, leisure, household tasks.

### 8. Ability, need, and obligation

**Prerequisite**

Comfort with `да + present` after `сакам`.

**Abilities**

- Say what someone can, must, or needs to do.
- Keep verb person aligned across common `да` constructions.
- Ask for and give simple practical help.

**Core patterns**

Introduce common Macedonian expressions for ability, necessity, and obligation one at a time, contrasting them with `сакам да`.

**Vocabulary themes**

Errands, work, health, travel preparation.

### 9. Future plans

**Prerequisite**

Reasonably stable present-person forms.

**Abilities**

- Talk about concrete future plans using `ќе + present`.
- Contrast a present routine with a future plan.
- Ask and answer what someone will do.

**Vocabulary themes**

Weekend plans, travel, social activities, upcoming tasks.

### 10. Past events

**Prerequisite**

Present persons, basic noun gender, and future-plan conversations are reasonably stable.

**Abilities**

- Describe a small number of completed past events.
- Ask simple questions about yesterday or a recent activity.
- Expand gradually because Macedonian past forms interact with gender and person.

**Vocabulary themes**

Yesterday, meals, outings, work and leisure events.

### 11. Verb aspect and object pronouns

**Prerequisite**

Comfort producing longer present, future, and basic past sentences.

**Abilities**

- Begin distinguishing ongoing or habitual actions from bounded or completed actions.
- Understand why forms such as habitual `купувам` may differ from a single intended purchase.
- Introduce direct and indirect object clitics in small, high-frequency patterns.

**Vocabulary themes**

Shopping, giving, seeing, telling, and completing tasks.

## Choosing the next normal lesson

Use this order of evidence:

1. Reconciliation markers and dated evidence agree.
2. Weekly cumulative review is not due; if it is due, run that instead.
3. Select at least one recently practised item for consolidation and at least one weak, stale, or overdue item for retrieval, using the tracker recency watchlists.
4. Confirm that both categories will appear in Review, Retrieval, and later mixed Practice or Conversation.
5. Select one syllabus competency at the learner's current edge.
6. Confirm its prerequisites from tracker evidence.
7. Preselect at least two genuinely new, related base words.
8. Design the stretch prompt and coherent final conversation before beginning.
9. Deliver one prompt at a time and adapt difficulty from actual responses.

## Evidence for advancing

Advance or broaden a competency when the learner has:

- produced it correctly without a construction hint on more than one occasion;
- used it in at least two task formats, such as translation and transformation;
- retrieved it after a delay rather than only immediately after explanation; and
- used it in a short exchange or mixed sentence.

These are guidelines for lesson selection, not statuses to record here. All dated evidence and status changes belong in the vocabulary and grammar trackers.
